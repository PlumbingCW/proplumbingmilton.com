Emergency steps

What to do when a toilet overflows onto the bathroom floor

Dark water or water that smells like sewage is a health matter, so keep children and pets out. Call us any hour, nights and weekends included, and do these things while you wait.

  1. Stop flushing. Each flush sends another tank of water toward a bowl that cannot drain.
  2. Lift the tank lid and press the rubber flapper down firmly to stop water entering the bowl.
  3. Turn the supply valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops. It may take several full turns.
  4. If the valve is stuck, lift the float arm and hold it up with a pencil laid across the tank so the fill valve stays closed.
  5. Lay towels around the base and move rugs and cabinet contents out of the wet area.
  6. If other drains gurgle or the tub shows water, stop using every fixture. That pattern points to the main line, not the toilet.

Why a toilet keeps running

Inside the tank, a flapper is a rubber disc that closes a two-inch outlet. Age and chlorine soften it until it stops sealing, and the fill valve then refills the lost water all day. A stuck fill valve is different: it never shuts off, and the excess spills down the overflow tube with a steady hiss. Both faults add quietly to your water bill.

Water at the base of the toilet

A wax ring sits between the toilet horn and the closet flange, the pipe fitting screwed to the floor. A damp floor, a rocking bowl, a ceiling stain below or a faint sewer smell all point here. Tightening bolts rarely solves it. The honest fix is to lift the toilet, check the flange and the subfloor, and set a new seal.

How a full toilet replacement goes

Replacing a toilet takes a plumber less time than most people expect, but the order matters.

  1. Shut off and drain

    The supply valve is closed, the toilet is flushed, and the leftover water is sponged out of the tank and bowl.

  2. Disconnect and lift

    The supply line and base nuts come off, and the old toilet is lifted away and wrapped for disposal.

  3. Inspect the flange

    The old wax is scraped off. The flange and the floor around it are checked for cracks, rust and soft wood.

  4. Set the new seal and bowl

    A new wax or wax-free seal goes on, and the bowl is lowered straight onto the closet bolts.

  5. Tighten gently

    Base nuts are tightened a little at a time on alternate sides, because over-tightening cracks the porcelain.

  6. Hang the tank and test

    The tank is mounted, a new supply line is connected, and the toilet is flushed several times while the base is watched for any seep.

Permits for toilet work in Massachusetts

Under the Massachusetts Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00, only a licensed master or journeyman plumber may do plumbing work. Minor repairs, such as replacing a flapper or clearing a blocked drain, do not need a permit. Replacing fixtures or piping may, and the permit comes from the local plumbing inspector.

Check any plumber's licence in the state eLIPSE portal. For what your Milton job requires, ask the Milton plumbing inspector. The Board of State Examiners publishes these permit guidelines, and your town's plumbing inspector applies them.

What affects the price of toilet work

The plumber gives a clear price before work starts. These are the drivers.

Part or whole fixture

A flapper, fill valve or handle costs far less than a new toilet, so the diagnosis sets the bracket.

Flange and floor condition

A cracked flange or soft subfloor adds materials and time, and it can only be seen once the toilet is up.

Fixture you choose

Comfort height, one-piece and wall-hung models differ in price and in how long they take to set.

Hour of the call

Night and weekend dispatch costs more than a scheduled daytime visit, since the crew is called out of hours.
