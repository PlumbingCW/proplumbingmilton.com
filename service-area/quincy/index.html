Before we arrive: burst pipe or active leak in Quincy

In a Quincy apartment building one leak can reach three floors. Do these four things quickly and then call the line.

Close the nearest valve

Look under the sink, behind the toilet or at the unit shutoff first. If the water is coming from a ceiling, close the unit valve or the main in the basement. Tell the dispatcher which one you closed.

Power off near water

Water dripping through a light fixture is a shock risk. Switch the breaker for that room off at the panel if the floor around the panel is dry. If it is not, stay away and say so when you call.

Protect the floors below

Move rugs and electronics, set buckets under drips, and knock on neighbors' doors if water is heading down. Photos of the stain and the source help with your insurer.

Call the 24/7 line

Give the building type, floor, and whether you can reach the owner or manager. Dispatch can then plan for access to a shared stack or basement before the plumber arrives.

How a property manager can arrange service

Managers keep control of access and records with a few steps.

  1. Collect the facts

    Note the units affected, the symptom and the last time the stack was cleaned.

  2. Call and describe the building

    Give the address, floors, and who holds the keys to units and the basement.

  3. Approve the clear price

    The plumber states the scope and price before work starts. Approve it in writing for the owner's records.

  4. Ask for a follow-up plan

    If a camera shows damage, ask for repair options and a cleaning schedule for the stack.
