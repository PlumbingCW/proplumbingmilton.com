Before we arrive: burst pipe or active leak in Randolph

A pipe that lets go can put gallons on the floor every minute. Spend the first few minutes on these four moves and then call.

Shut the water at the main

Walk to where the line enters the house, usually the basement wall or a utility room. Close the valve fully. If it will not turn, do not force it with a wrench. Tell dispatch and we will plan for a stuck valve.

Cut power if wet

Water running through a ceiling can reach light fixtures and wiring. If lights flicker or you see water at outlets, turn off that circuit from a dry panel. Skip the panel if the floor around it is wet.

Clear a dry path

Pull furniture and valuables away from the wet zone and set buckets under drips. Lift anything on the floor above the waterline. Open a cabinet door under the sink if the leak is there.

Call the 24/7 line

The meter keeps moving and nothing is running

That is the classic sign of a hidden leak. A pinhole in a copper line, a failed joint in a wall or a crack in a buried service line can all lose water for weeks before a stain shows. Leak detection finds the spot without tearing out half a room.

Start with the checks below. If they come up empty, the pipe is probably out of sight, and listening gear is the faster route.

Five checks before you call about a high bill

Do these in order. Each one rules out a common culprit.

  • Close the main, read the meter, wait twenty minutes and read it again.
  • Drop a few dye drops in each toilet tank and wait to see if color reaches the bowl.
  • Look under every sink and behind the washer for damp cabinet floors.
  • Check the ceiling below any bathroom for brown rings or soft drywall.
  • Listen at the water heater for a steady hiss or the sound of running water.

Why household pipes get the blame in Randolph

Randolph is not on MWRA water. The Randolph-Holbrook Joint Water System, run by the two towns, draws from the Great Pond Reservoir System. The 2024 report found one of 30 sites over the lead action level and one site over the copper action level, and attributed both to corrosion of household plumbing. For you, that means the pipes and solder inside the house matter more than the main in the street.

The same report lists hardness at 39.6 ppm from a single sample and sodium at 94.9 ppm, above the 20 ppm guidance for people on sodium-restricted diets. What a plumber can and cannot say about your water
