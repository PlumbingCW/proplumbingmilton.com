Before we arrive: burst pipe or active leak in Canton

Water can ruin a Canton basement in minutes. These four steps slow the damage while the plumber is on the way, and none of them needs more than a flashlight.

Find the main shutoff

In most Canton houses the main valve sits where the water line comes into the basement, close to the meter. Turn a wheel handle clockwise until it stops. A lever handle is closed when it lies across the pipe.

Keep power away from water

If water is near outlets, the furnace or the panel, stay out of it. Switch breakers off only from a dry spot. Never touch a sump pump cord while you stand in water.

Lift what you can

Move boxes, rugs and electronics onto shelves or a table. Getting stored items up even one foot can save them. Take photos of the damage first, because your insurer will ask.

Call the 24/7 line

Call once the flow has slowed. Tell dispatch where the water is coming from, what the pipe looks like, and whether the main is off. That helps the plumber bring the right parts.

Water heater or sump pump failing? Check this first

Three quick looks give the dispatcher more to work with.

  1. Check the base of the tank

    Water pooling under a tank usually means the tank has failed. Close the cold supply valve above the heater to stop the flow.

  2. Smell for gas

    If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 or your gas utility from outside before you call a plumber.

  3. Look into the sump pit

    If the water is near the rim and the pump is silent, check the plug and the breaker. Do not reach in while it is powered.
