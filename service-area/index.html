Inside Milton: the neighborhoods the line covers

Milton Village sits on the Neponset River at the Boston line, and the river banks there are where the Town's 2020 resilience report places much of its flooding. For a homeowner that makes a working sump pump and a clear sewer cleanout worth checking before heavy rain. East Milton Square, near Quincy, is the area the same report describes as having storm surge back sewers up into homes, so a backwater valve question belongs in any basement plumbing conversation there.

Milton Hill, Brush Hill and western Milton along Canton Avenue hold much of the town's older housing, including large historic estates. Older houses tend to have older shutoff valves and cast iron drains, so the plumber may suggest replacing a valve that has not turned in decades before it fails during a repair.

Water sources differ from town to town

Milton and Quincy buy their water from the MWRA. Canton uses a mix of MWRA water and its own groundwater wells, and the mix has changed over the years. Randolph is not on the MWRA at all and runs the Randolph-Holbrook Joint Water System, and Braintree draws from its own surface supply. The practical effect is that a plumber cannot assume the same water chemistry from one town to the next.

For any question about taste, color or whether water is safe to drink, the utility's annual water quality report is the right source. A plumber can fix the pipes in your house, but only the utility or a laboratory test can tell you what is in the water. According to each utility's own annual report, that is where the numbers live.
