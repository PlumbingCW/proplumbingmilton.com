Before we arrive: burst pipe or active leak in Braintree

A split pipe in a cold basement can run all night before anyone notices. If it is happening now, work through these four steps.

Turn off the main supply

Follow the line from the meter to the first valve inside the house. Close it all the way. If the pipe is frozen and has split, the leak may start only as it thaws, so keep the valve closed until the plumber says otherwise.

Stay clear of live circuits

If water is near the furnace, the washer or the panel, do not stand in it. Turn off the breaker only with dry hands on a dry floor. Otherwise tell dispatch and leave the panel alone.

Move valuables up and out

Carry stored boxes, rugs and electronics off the floor. Open basement windows or doors only if it is safe, and use towels to guide water to a floor drain.

Freezing weather: split pipes and stuck valves

Pipes in unheated garages, crawl spaces and exterior walls freeze first. Ice splits the wall, and the leak often starts only when the ice melts. A faucet that runs slowly in freezing weather is a warning, so call before the pipe opens.

A burst pipe repair begins with the main valve and a thaw plan, then the cut and the fitting. Outdoor spigots need to be shut from inside the house before freezing weather, and hoses must be off.

Spring wet season: sump pits and discharge lines

Snowmelt and long rains raise the water table, and a sump pump that sat idle all winter may jam. Test the pump now by pouring a bucket of water into the pit and watching the float rise.

Braintree adds one rule. The Water and Sewer Department says sump pumps must discharge away from the sanitary sewer, and it inspects setups at no charge. A sump pump that dumps into a sewer cleanout can add to street backups. Ask the department at 781-843-8097 before rerouting, since it also ran an amnesty period for rerouting.

Summer storms: backflow, hoses and overwhelmed drains

Short, heavy storms test floor drains and basement stairwells. Check that the cover is clear and the trap holds water. Put a backflow preventer on each hose spigot, as the Braintree water report advises.
