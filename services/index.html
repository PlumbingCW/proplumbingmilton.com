How to pick the right page

Start with the symptom, not the name of the repair. A damp ceiling, a bill that doubled and a meter that moves with every tap off all point to a hidden leak, so leak detection is the page to read. Water backing up into a tub when the washer drains points to the main line, which is where sewer line cleaning and sewer inspection come in.

Heat problems split the same way. A tank that is cold or leaking belongs with water heater repair or replacement. A house that has lost heat in winter belongs with boiler repair, because roughly a quarter of Massachusetts homes heat with a steam or hot-water boiler according to the 2020 federal residential energy survey.

What every service page includes

Each page opens with the urgent version of the problem where one exists, then explains the repair, a comparison table, the cost drivers, local notes and a set of questions people ask before they call. Cost sections list what moves the price and never a made-up range, because the honest number comes from looking at your pipes.

Pages that cover emergencies carry a short list of steps to take while you wait. Shutting off the main valve, stopping use of drains and keeping people away from wet electrical areas are the three that come up most.
