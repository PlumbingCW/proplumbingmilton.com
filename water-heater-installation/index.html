What happens on install day

  1. Walk the site

    The plumber checks the room, the shutoffs, the vent path and the electrical panel before touching anything.

  2. Pull the permit

    A licensed plumber obtains the permit from the local plumbing inspector. You can ask to see it.

  3. Set the base

    A drain pan goes under the new unit, with a pipe to a drain or an outdoor point.

  4. Connect water, fuel and vent

    Supply lines, relief valve piping, gas or electrical connections and the flue are made up and tested.

  5. Fill and commission

    The tank is filled and purged, the controls are set, and the plumber shows you the shutoff and the temperature setting.

Gas lines, wiring and who is licensed for what

A gas heater needs a gas supply sized for its burner, a shutoff valve at the appliance, and a sediment trap on the line. An electric or heat pump unit needs a circuit sized for its load, and the breaker must match the wire. These are different trades, and each has its own licence.

Be direct when you call. Ask who will do the gas fitting, who handles the wiring, and who pulls each permit. A plumber who says the whole job is theirs should be able to explain which license covers which piece. You can check any plumber on the state's eLIPSE portal or by calling the Board of State Examiners at 617-727-9952.

Those licence lines come from the Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters, and 248 CMR 10.00 is the standard the work is held to.

Venting and combustion air

A gas storage heater exhausts hot gases through a flue that must slope up and stay sealed. Tight, modern basements can starve a burner of combustion air, which risks back drafting. The plumber looks at the room volume and the vent path to see if an air supply opening is needed.

If you are moving a heater in an older Milton house, the old chimney flue may be too large for a modern unit. With a median home age of 71 years in town, many houses have chimneys that were built for oil or coal. A liner or a new side wall vent may be the cleaner solution, and the plumbing inspector can say what the town expects.

New construction and additions

In a new build or addition, the heater location is set during rough in, when pipes and vents are still open. Put it close to the bathrooms and kitchen to cut wait time at the tap, and leave room for service access. Heat pump and tankless plans should be settled before the walls close. What affects the price of an installation

The plumber quotes a clear price before work starts. The usual drivers are below.

Unit type

Tankless and heat pump models need more parts and more setup than a basic tank.

Fuel changes

Switching from gas to electric, or the reverse, means new lines, circuits or venting.

Distance moved

Longer pipe, vent and wire runs raise materials and labor.

Permits and inspections

Permit work and any second trade, such as an electrician, are part of the scope.

Removal and cleanup

Confirm whether the old unit's disposal is included.

Planned upgrades are usually not covered by homeowners insurance. Sudden water damage from a failed heater may be, so read your policy or ask your insurer.
