Hydro jetting is a drain cleaning method that sends high-pressure water through a hose and a special nozzle to scrape and flush the inside of a pipe. It removes grease, scale, silt and root cuttings that a cable leaves behind. We offer it as a 24/7 emergency service in Milton, MA when a main line is backing up. While you wait for the plumber, stop using water and keep drains closed.

Questions people ask before calling about hydro jetting

The line clogs again a few weeks after snaking

A repeat clog usually means the first visit opened a hole but left the coating on the pipe wall. Grease, soap film and scale grow back on that rough surface, and the opening shrinks again. Hydro jetting is the method designed to clean the wall itself, and it starts with a look at what the pipe is made of.

Grease and roots: the two jobs jetting does best

Grease behaves like candle wax. It cools inside the pipe, grabs hair and food particles, and builds a ring that narrows the flow. A cable can open that ring in the middle. A jet nozzle with rear-facing streams pulls itself forward and strips the ring from all sides.

Roots find their way into joints and cracks looking for moisture. Fine hair roots wash out with water. Thick root masses need a blade first. After the pipe is clear, a camera shows where the roots entered, which tells you whether the pipe has to be repaired.

When jetting is the wrong tool

Jetting is not a cure for every slow drain. Water pressure that cleans a healthy pipe can finish off a pipe that is already failing. Be cautious in these cases:

  • The line is old cast iron that is flaking, or thin galvanized steel that is rusted through.
  • A cable stopped at the same spot twice, which can mean a collapse, an offset joint or a foreign object.
  • Clay pipe with joints that have shifted, where pressure can push water into the soil.
  • The pipe is Orangeburg or other fiber material that softens over time.
  • The blockage is a single hard object, which a retrieval tool handles better.

Why older Milton houses get a camera first

The Town of Milton Housing Production Plan counts about three quarters of the town's roughly 9,700 homes as built before 1960. Houses from that era often have drain materials that age differently from modern PVC. That is the reason a camera look before pressure is a sensible step on an older line, and a sewer camera inspection gives you a recording to keep.

How a jetting visit goes

  1. Find the entry

    The plumber opens the cleanout or pulls a fixture to get the hose into the line.

  2. Look before pushing

    A camera or a test with a small cable checks for breaks, offsets and heavy root masses.

  3. Choose nozzle and pressure

    Pipe size and material set the nozzle type and how hard the water runs.

  4. Feed and pull back

    The hose runs forward, then is pulled back slowly so the nozzle cleans each foot twice.

  5. Check the result

    Water is run to confirm full flow, and the camera shows the cleaned wall.
Emergency steps

When a backed-up main line needs jetting tonight

If sewage is coming up in the lowest drain, toilets are bubbling, or several fixtures are rising together, call at any hour. We dispatch nights, weekends and holidays, and a main line backup is a same-night problem. Here is what is safe to do now:

  1. Stop all water use, including washers, dishwashers and showers.
  2. Do not flush. Every flush sends more water toward the lowest drain.
  3. Keep people and pets out of standing water and use gloves if you must clean up.
  4. Shut off power to the basement only if you can reach the switch without standing in water. Otherwise stay out and call 911.
  5. Open the cleanout cap only if you know where it is and can stand clear, since pressure may push wastewater out.
