Boiler repair covers the diagnosis and fixing of a heating boiler that heats water or makes steam for radiators, baseboards or in-floor loops. Common faults include no heat, banging pipes, a leak, a pressure gauge that reads too high or too low, and radiators that stay cold at the top. Questions people ask before calling about a boiler

Air trapped in a hot-water radiator is the most common cause, and bleeding it is a safe homeowner step. If every radiator is cold, the cause may be a failed circulator pump, a closed valve, low system pressure or a thermostat that is not calling for heat. A plumber can sort out which.

Many residential hot-water boilers sit near 12 to 15 psi when cold, but your boiler's label and manual decide. A reading near zero suggests a leak or lost water. A reading that climbs toward 30 psi means the relief valve may open, so shut the boiler off and call.

Licensed plumbers handle the water side, including piping, valves, pumps and expansion tanks. Work that touches gas piping or burners involves gas fitting licences. What the symptoms usually point to

Boilers tend to fail in recognizable ways. Matching the symptom to a likely cause gives you a better call to the plumber and a faster fix.

  • No heat and no hot water: a tripped switch, a lockout, a dead ignition or no fuel.
  • Heat in some rooms only: trapped air, a closed zone valve or a failing circulator.
  • Banging or gurgling: air in the loop, scale, or trapped water in steam piping.
  • Puddle under the boiler: a weeping valve, a failed gasket, or a corroded heat exchanger.
  • Gauge that keeps rising: a failed expansion tank or a feed valve stuck open.
  • Pressure that keeps falling: a leak somewhere in the system, including inside walls.

Bleeding air from a hot-water radiator

Do this only on a hot-water system, never steam. Turn the heat on, let the radiators warm, then turn the boiler off before you start.

  1. Find the valve

    It is a small square or slotted fitting near the top corner of the radiator.

  2. Hold a cloth and cup

    Water may spit out after the air, and it can be hot.

  3. Open a quarter turn

    Use a radiator key. A hiss means air is leaving.

  4. Close when water appears

    Shut the valve as soon as a steady stream of water follows the hiss.

  5. Recheck the gauge

    Bleeding drops system pressure, so confirm the gauge is back in its normal range.

Why Milton houses and boilers go together

The 2020 EIA Residential Energy Consumption Survey found that 26% of Massachusetts homes heat with a steam or hot-water boiler, while 55% use furnaces. In Milton, where 46.8% of homes were built in 1939 or earlier and the median home is 71 years old, many houses were built around radiators. If your home has them, your boiler may be older than the typical statewide unit, so treat noise and rust as early warnings.

Licensing and permits for heating work

Plumbing work in Massachusetts must be done by a licensed plumber, and gas fitting has its own licence under the Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters. Heating work that touches gas piping or burners therefore involves gas fitting licences, and some jobs also involve other trades.

Ask the plumber who is licensed for what, whether a permit is required, and to see it. You can check any licence in the state eLIPSE portal. For local specifics, ask the Milton plumbing inspector. The standard that governs this work is the Massachusetts Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00, and gas fitting has its own licence under the same Board.

What affects the price of boiler repair

The plumber gives a clear price before work starts. Several things move it.

The fault

Bleeding a loop is a minor visit, while a pump or heat exchanger is a larger job.

System type

Steam systems have different parts than hot-water ones.

Parts availability

Older boilers may need parts that take time to find.

Gas or burner work

Gas fitting work adds licensed labor and testing.

Age and condition

A boiler near the end of its life may need repair or a replacement plan.

Homeowners policies often cover sudden and accidental damage but usually not wear or maintenance. Policy wording decides, so read it or ask your insurer.
