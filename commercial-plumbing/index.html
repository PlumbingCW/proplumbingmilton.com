Floor drain backing up in the shop or kitchen?

Water that rises in a floor drain means the line downstream is blocked or overloaded. A shared floor drain can also bring up water when another fixture, such as a restroom or mop sink, discharges into the same branch. The first step is to stop sending water down the drains. Then call us so the plumber can trace the blockage, clear it and tell you whether the pipe needs repair.

Most of this work is drain cleaning at larger pipe sizes, scheduled around your business hours.

Emergency steps

Restroom out of service or water coming through the ceiling

Dispatch is 24/7, nights and weekends included, and a closed restroom costs a business every hour. Do these things while you wait.

  1. Close the fixture's own shutoff, then the area valve, then the building main if water is still moving.
  2. Keep customers, staff and tenants out of the wet area, and put out wet-floor signs.
  3. Switch off power to affected equipment only if you can reach the panel without standing in water.
  4. Write down which fixtures are affected, the time it began, and photograph the damage for your records.
  5. If sewage is backing up, do not use any fixture and tell the property manager.

Commercial leak detection: what to expect

Finding a leak in a small shop can take a short visit. In a large building, or on lines under a slab or buried between the street and the building, it can take more. The method uses acoustic listening, pressure testing and a camera, and results depend on the piping, access and noise.

Ask three things before you agree: what is in scope, what happens if the first visit does not pinpoint the leak, and how pricing works. Also ask what tools the plumber brings. An honest plumber explains the limits up front.

Grease trap cleaning for restaurants and kitchens

A grease trap holds fat, oil and food solids before wastewater enters the drain. It must be pumped and cleaned on a schedule, and the right interval depends on how much the kitchen cooks. Skip it, and grease coats the pipes, causes slow drains and odors, and can back up into the dining area.

Local grease rules vary by town and sewer utility. Ask the Milton Water and Sewer Division at 617-696-5731 or the Town about requirements for your type of business. Grease rules are set locally, so ask the Town and the sewer authority which standard applies to your kitchen.

Floor drain overflow and lift pump failures

Below-grade restrooms and basement floor drains often depend on a lift pump to raise wastewater to the sewer. When the float jams or the pump burns out, the basin fills and the drain overflows. A plumber checks the float, the check valve and the discharge line. Replacement is likely when the pump is old or cycles constantly.

Milton's flood history is concentrated along the Neponset River, so a below-grade drain in a low area carries storm-time risk. Ask about a high-water alarm and a backup plan. Our sump pump page covers related equipment.

What drives the cost of commercial plumbing

The plumber gives a clear price before work starts. These are the drivers.

Building size and pipe size

Larger drains and longer runs need heavier equipment and more time.

Access and hours

Work in a busy kitchen, or on a slab, takes longer. After-hours dispatch costs more than a scheduled daytime visit.

Scope of leak location

A large site may need more than one visit to confirm a hidden leak, so scope affects the final bill.

Recurring service

A grease trap on a schedule is a planned cost. An emergency clear-out is not.

Repair after diagnosis

Clearing a line is one price. Repairing a broken section is another.

Commercial property policies often cover sudden and accidental damage but usually not wear, gradual leaks or maintenance. Policy wording decides, so read yours or ask the insurer.
