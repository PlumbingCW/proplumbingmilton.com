A tankless water heater, also called an on-demand heater, warms water only while a tap is open, using a gas burner or electric elements and a heat exchanger. There is no storage tank to run empty. Hot water runs out in back to back showers, or the unit shows an error code

Both problems usually trace to the same place: flow, scale or fuel. A tankless heater can only heat as many gallons per minute as its burner or elements allow, so two showers plus a dishwasher can exceed the rating. An error code often means the heat exchanger is coated with minerals or the vent has an obstruction.

A plumber who works on tankless water heater systems starts with the flow rate, the incoming temperature, the gas pressure and the code history. Tell the plumber what happens, when it happens and what the display says, and the visit goes much faster.

The cold water sandwich, explained

When you open a tap, the heater has to detect flow before it fires. Water already in the pipe between the heater and the tap is whatever temperature it cooled to. After you shut a tap and reopen it quickly, the heater may still hold a pocket of cold water that arrives between two warm streams.

Several things reduce it. Keeping a tap open a bit longer, moving the heater closer to the fixtures, adding a small buffer tank or a recirculation setup, and using fixtures with a flow above the unit's minimum all help. None of these is a magic cure, so ask what fits your piping.

Flushing and descaling a tankless heater

A flush circulates a mild acid solution, often white vinegar or a manufacturer approved descaler, through the heat exchanger using a small pump and two hoses attached to service valves. The solution dissolves mineral scale. Then clean water rinses it out. Without isolation valves on the unit, the plumber has to add them first.

Why it matters: scale is a thin, stony layer that acts as insulation. The heat exchanger has to burn longer for the same temperature, flow drops, error codes appear and parts wear faster. The listed trade-offs are general. Your fuel, plumbing and habits decide the outcome.

Gas line and venting considerations

Gas tankless models draw a lot of fuel in a short time. The gas pipe that fed a tank may be too small once the run length and other appliances are counted. The plumber has to size the gas line to the unit's maximum input, and gas fitting work involves its own licensing, so ask who is licensed to do it.

Exhaust is the second item. Many units vent through a small diameter pipe made of a specific material, run to an outside wall. Condensing models also need a drain for acidic condensate. Vent length, bends and termination height all come from the manufacturer's instructions. Getting the vent wrong is a safety issue, not a cosmetic one. According to the Board's policy statement, installs must follow the manufacturer's instructions and sizing guides, so ask any installer which guide they used.
