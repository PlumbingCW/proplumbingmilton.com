Cartridge, washer or ball: what is inside your faucet

The repair depends on the faucet's inner design. Here is how the common types compare.

Faucet typeHow it stops waterCommon failureRepair
Compression (two handles, washers)A rubber washer presses on a seatDrip from the spout, hard handleNew washer and seat, often cheap
Cartridge (single or two handle)A sleeve slides or turns to open portsDrip, stiff or loose handle, weak flowReplace the cartridge and O-rings
Ball (older single handle)A slotted ball rotates over seatsLeak around the base, dripReplace the seats, springs and ball
Ceramic discTwo polished discs slide togetherSlow leak, mineral buildup in the discsClean or replace the disc cartridge

Many modern faucets fit a cartridge sold by the maker. Have the faucet brand handy when you call.

Where your shutoff valves are

Knowing these four spots turns a flood into a mop-up job.

  • Under the sink: two small oval or round valves on the supply lines, one hot and one cold.
  • Behind the toilet: one valve at the wall on the supply tube.
  • At appliances: washing machine hoses and the dishwasher supply have their own valves.
  • At the main: where the pipe enters the house, usually in a basement or utility area, often near the meter.
Emergency steps

What to do when a supply line bursts or a valve will not shut off

Water under pressure is the real emergency here. We dispatch 24/7, and a gushing line at night is a call we take. Use these steps until the plumber arrives.

  1. Turn off the nearest shutoff valve clockwise. If it spins freely or will not turn, stop forcing it.
  2. Go to the main shutoff and close it. If the main also sticks, the Town can help with the curb stop, since the Town maintains the line from the street main to the curb stop.
  3. Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain pressure from the pipes.
  4. Switch off power to anything wet near the leak, but only if you can reach the breaker while dry.
  5. Soak up standing water and photograph the damage for your records.

When the shutoff valve itself is the problem

Old gate valves have a threaded stem that corrodes in place. They leak at the handle, refuse to close or snap off when turned. Oil and a bigger wrench is not the answer. The plumber closes the water upstream, cuts the old valve out and fits a quarter-turn ball valve.

In Milton, the property owner maintains the line from the curb stop to the home, so a failed main shutoff inside is yours to replace. If the street-side valve will not close, call the Milton Water and Sewer Division.

Tub and shower diverter problems

A diverter sends water to the tub spout or up to the showerhead. When it wears out, water keeps pouring from the spout while the shower runs, or the shower sprays weakly. A pull-up diverter on the spout can fail from a worn gasket. A valve with a three-handle design needs a new diverter cartridge.

Showerhead drips after use usually mean a worn cartridge or valve seat behind the wall. Water at the ceiling below points the same way. A repair there often needs access through the wall.

How a faucet repair visit goes

  1. Shut off and test

    The plumber closes the local stop, opens the faucet and checks the supply lines and handles for stiffness.

  2. Identify the type

    The handle comes off to reveal a washer, cartridge, ball or disc. The brand sets the correct part.

  3. Replace the worn parts

    Seals, seats and cartridges are replaced, and mineral buildup is cleaned out of the aerator and body.

  4. Check the stops

    The shutoffs underneath are tested. A seeping stop is replaced at the same visit, with a permit where one is required.

  5. Run and watch

    The faucet runs hot and cold while the plumber watches every joint for a bead of moisture.

Slow shower or tub drain

A tub that drains slowly is usually hair and soap sitting in the trap or the stopper linkage. Pulling the stopper and cleaning the strainer often helps. If the water backs up elsewhere or the fix does not last, the line past the trap has a clog, and a drain cleaning clears it.

Repair rules in Massachusetts and older Milton plumbing

The Massachusetts Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00, says only licensed plumbers may do plumbing work. A leaky faucet repair needs no permit, but replacing or relocating a faucet or valve does. Milton's median home is 71 years old, and many were built before 1960, so original shutoffs and galvanized stubs are common. Ask the plumber to check them while the faucet is open, and consider repiping if the supply pipes are failing.

These are the Board's published guidelines, and the local plumbing inspector applies them.

What affects the price of faucet repair

The plumber gives a clear price before work starts. These are the drivers.

Repair or replace

A washer or cartridge swap costs less than a new faucet, and a failing old faucet may be cheaper to replace.

Parts availability

Common cartridges are easy to get. Specialty or imported fixtures can need ordering, which can mean a second visit.

Wall access

A shower valve behind tile needs opening the wall or working from the back side, which adds labor.

Stuck shutoffs

When the local stop will not close, the plumber works from the main, and replacing the stop adds a part and a permit.

Hour of the call

Emergency dispatch at night costs more than a scheduled visit during the day.

Many homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage but usually not wear, gradual leaks or maintenance. Policy wording decides, so read yours or ask the insurer.
